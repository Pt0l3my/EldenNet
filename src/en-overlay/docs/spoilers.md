# Spoilers: how EldenNet keeps them

A character should never tell you what lies ahead. EldenNet keeps spoilers out by what it gives the model, not by
lists of what a character must not say.

## Each character knows what they would know

Every request EldenNet sends for a character holds only what that character knows at the point your journey has
reached, in EldenNet's own words. Nothing in it names what the character must keep quiet about, and nothing in it
tells the story ahead.

- **At a first meeting**, a character knows what anyone could see of them, their own manner and temperament, and what
  they would tell a stranger about themselves.
- **Later**, a profile may hold further stages, each tied to a sign in the game that your journey has reached it.
  EldenNet uses such a sign only once it has been checked in the game itself. Until then the stage stays hidden: a sign
  that has not been checked can keep a stage hidden, never reveal one.
- **The world** works the same way: what a character knows of the state of the world follows the same checked signs.

In this version no sign has been checked yet, so every character speaks from what they would know at a first meeting.

## What the model may know

The AI model is not EldenNet. A model may have read about the game, and EldenNet cannot take that away. EldenNet's
requests never tell the model the story ahead, and ask it to speak as a person of that world who knows only their own
part of it. Nothing checks a reply for spoilers, though: a model can still say more than the character should. If one
does, that came from the model, not from the character's profile. Pressing Esc closes the box.

## On the pages

- The pages show only what the game has shown you, or what EldenNet wrote to be read by anyone: a character's general
  profile, EldenNet's own labels and help.
- **Characters › Named NPCs** lists every character EldenNet voices from the start, by the name you would know them by
  at a first meeting, with their general profile: what anyone would see on meeting them. It is the one list that shows
  characters you may not have met, by design.
- Nothing is counted, greyed or hinted at that you have not reached.
- Search, page titles and the browser's addresses never carry story or conversation text.
- The developer view (F11) is off unless you turn on Developer views. It shows EldenNet's own numbers and words, never
  the story.
- These documents name no person, place or event of the game.

## No spoiler setting

There is no setting for spoilers: these rules always apply, and nothing turns them off.
