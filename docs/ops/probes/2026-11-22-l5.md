## What it settled

1. **In the game, the finder finds what it found in the file.** The accessor's shape stands at 7,077 places, all
   accepted, and gives 217 statics with 217 class objects and one getter. Every static, its class object, its count of
   sites and its witnesses are the offline run's, value for value (`2026-11-22-l3.md`); so are the two character
   classes' sets (6 tables of methods and 2), the task groups' table (168 entries of 32 bytes, the lead's group at
   index 110) and the withdrawn pattern's numbers (7,151 hits, 7,081 accepted, 4 sites the shape leaves out). The image
   as loaded differs from the file in nothing the finder reads.
2. **Eight of the eleven managers are named, each by its instance's class, and none twice.** Six at the first pass,
   at the title: `task_hub`, `session`, `menus`, `loading`, `camera`, `param_store`. Two at pass 56, some nine and a half minutes
   in, LIKELY the first pass after the save was loaded: `characters` and `hud`. Each was named by the lead's name with Imp. Each is one of the
   217 statics, with the accessor's own count of sites for it (from 7 to 896), and no two share a table of methods.
3. **Three are not named: `event_flags`, `world_clock` and `messages`.** At the last pass (the 60th, LIKELY some 40 s into
   the world) 139 of the 217 statics held an instance whose class the image's type information gives, 71 held an
   object whose first word is no table of methods the image knows, 6 were empty and 1 could not be read. So either
   those three managers are among the 71 (objects with no type information, which O92's way cannot name), or their
   classes go by other names than the leads', or they are among the 6 not yet made. The report cannot say which: it
   does not count, for a lead, whether the image holds a class of its name at all. That count is the next thing to add.
4. **The round stopped naming at its cap.** It names every 10 s for at most 60 passes (10 minutes), and the two world
   managers were first named at pass 56, so the world LIKELY had 5 passes. A manager made later than that would have been missed. The
   cap is to go: the round should name for as long as a lead is missing.
5. **The reader read for 19 minutes with no fault.** From a thread of our own, 10.0 readings a second (11,552 in
   1,155 s, in 33,475 steps): 242,244 guarded copies and not one refused or faulted, of any kind. No reading saw a
   slot change between its two reads, and no reading saw an object of another class behind a named slot.
   - `task_hub`: its class at all 11,552 readings.
   - `session`, `menus`, `loading`, `camera`, `param_store`: their class at 11,426 to 11,441 readings, empty at 111
     to 126 (11 to 13 s in all).
   - `characters` and `hud`, read from pass 56 on: their class at 5,886 and 5,854 of 6,028 readings, empty at 142 and
     174.
   The empty readings are whole seconds in which the game had taken a manager away or not yet made it. When they fell
   (a loading screen, the return to the title, the game closing) the report does not say; it counts and does not time.
6. **A reading costs microseconds.** The median reading of all eight managers took 3 µs, the 99th percentile 6 µs,
   the longest 319 µs.
7. **The draft table has 11 entries, each checked against the image before it was written**: the eight managers
   (found by the accessor and by the instance's class, so two recipes agree; 7 or 8 witnesses each, every one still
   referring to its static), the two character classes (one recipe, so no agreement yet) and the task groups' table.
   Everything the round named is in it, and each manager's slot and witnesses in the table are the report's own.
   Every entry is a lead.

## The second round in game

- **Date**: 2026-11-22, the same PC and build, in the game, offline and without Easy Anti-Cheat. **How**: the package
  `EldenNet-Read.zip`, label 0.1.0-lodestar.5, built from `feat/lodestar` at 2ebeb19. Read-only as the first: no call,
  nothing registered, nothing written. Its searches ask before each read (VirtualQuery, then ReadProcessMemory). It
  ran for about 9 minutes, 6.6 of them with a save loaded (the trial now counts by stage).
- **SHA-256**: package `4fe39e7b0b099fe3e24ac2d4382f7d49d6c79e9da436ad09085d57bc1e4892d5`; eldennet.dll
  `2194339125369a2bf616d2219c9abad2077217c5fc45c9bfdd3cc3492be5ead3`; report
  `78a06310d71cd5f933c71a6052c02010982d8126be6c13aa69cfb030726da728`; draft table
  `9d66a5ed935dec8a336e5ebbc08057313990375d5b0d39b598bb6e94cccc9009`. Reviewed as the others; `lint --report` and the
  sweep find both clean. Only the two files came back, not the logs.

What it settled:

1. **The same eight managers, at the same places.** A second session of the game gives each of the eight the same
   static, the same table of methods and the same witnesses as the first, value for value, and the draft table is the
   first run's entry for entry. `characters` and `hud` were named at pass 5 this time, soon after the save loaded.
2. **The reader again met no fault**: 4,402 readings in 8.9 minutes, 102,864 guarded copies, none refused or faulted,
   no slot seen to change between its two reads, no object of another class behind a named slot. A reading took 3 µs
   at the median, 5 at the 99th percentile, 115 at the longest.
3. **When the managers were away.** The stage changed once (title to world). Six of the eight were empty in one
   stretch only, of 0.1 to 3.2 s, and never in the world: the game making them as it starts, or as the save loads.
   `task_hub` was never empty. `hud` was empty in five stretches, 2.7 s of them in the world. So a manager's slot
   going empty for seconds is the game's ordinary course, and the reader must take it as such.
4. **The instance inside the image was the static the first round would not read**: one static's object lies in the
   image; read through the image's view it has a class, and it is none of the leads'.
5. **`event_flags` and `messages` are still not named**, by the passes or, as far as the report says, by the search of
   the data's slots: no slot found for either. The report does not say how many slots each search went through, or
   whether it ended; the DLL's log does, and it was not sent. Until then the cause stays open. A lead from our own
   research notes fits `messages`: its accessors are said to have an instruction between the load and the test
   (docs/research/eldenring/03, a pattern another tool uses), which the accessor's shape does not allow, so its
   static would be none of the 217.
6. **The clock search found no clock.** Over the 209 statics no lead has named, about 780 passes: one field stepped
   forward 110 times within 0 to 59, never went round and has no hours beside it. That is a slow counter, not a
   clock's minutes. So the time of day is not kept as whole minutes and hours in the first 256 bytes of any object
   behind an accessor's static. It may be kept otherwise (as a fraction of a day, or in seconds), or elsewhere.
7. **The play time has one strong candidate.** Of 16,384 slots of the data that held a heap address, 13,960 pointed
   at memory the search left alone (the ask-first copy refused it) and 2,424 were read, 119 passes each. One 32-bit
   field rose by the clock's milliseconds in 107 passes, stood still in 4 and changed otherwise in 8; every other hit
   rose in 1 to 3 passes. Its slot is none of the accessor's statics. The search keeps the first 32 hits it meets, not
   the best, so a better one may have been dropped: it is to keep the best.
8. **The searches slowed the reader's thread**: 14 steps a second instead of 29, and 8.2 readings a second instead
   of 10. Asking before each read costs two system calls a candidate, and a slot search makes a thousand a step. The
   game's own threads are not touched by it, but the trial's rate in this run is the searches' doing, and the next
   round should give the searches a smaller share of each step.
