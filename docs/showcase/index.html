EldenNet

A mod for Elden Ring on PC, for offline play, that lets the game’s characters talk with you in their own words, remember you, and know what is going on around them.

  • Offline, solo play
  • Dialogue only in version 1
  • A cloud model or your own
  • Spoiler-safe by design
  • Never writes to your game

Design stage: waiting for the engine blueprint Where it stands

What it is

EldenNet gives the characters of Elden Ring a mind of their own, through a large language model that you choose: a cloud service with your own key, or a model running on your own computer. Named characters have a personality, know where they are and what is happening around them, remember you and each other, and can hold a free conversation with you in typed text. Everyone else with a voice, such as soldiers and wanderers, knows what they are and where they are, and says short lines in a fight and around it.

Version 1 is deliberately small and stable. Characters speak and do nothing else: no actions, no gestures, nothing changed in your game. EldenNet reads the game and never writes to it, and it stays dormant unless you play offline, alone in your own world.

EldenNet builds on SkyrimNet, Min’s AI framework for the characters of Skyrim, with Min’s approval. It is a project of its own, not SkyrimNet itself. Its menu and dashboard, GraceUI, is a mod of its own too, and every EldenNet release carries it.

What version 1 does

  • Talk with any named character the game lets you meet calmly: press the talk key, type, and they answer in character.
  • A profile for every named character and named boss of the base game, about 140 in all, written by us and reviewed by hand for spoilers.
  • They remember you, and the other characters they deal with, from one meeting to the next.
  • They know where things stand: their own story, the stories tied to it, and the state of the land, as the game itself records them.
  • They speak up on their own: greetings, quest business, news, remarks and exchanges between characters, held back only by the pacing you set.
  • Fights have voices: lines for every fighter on either side, boss fights included, never over the game’s own lines.
  • Your model, your choice: any service or local model server that speaks the common OpenAI-compatible protocol, with ready presets for both.
  • One menu, in game and in your browser: GraceUI opens on a hotkey in game, and in a browser on the same PC while the game runs.
  • Text first. Spoken voices and talking aloud come in version 1.5; actions and gestures in a later version.

Features

What the design holds today. Each card says where the design describes it, for anyone who wants the detail.

Conversation

Conversation with every profiled character

Walk up to a character the game lets you meet calmly, press the talk key and type. They answer in their own manner, in short pages paced for reading. The game never pauses: if danger comes, the chat box closes and hands your controls back at once. EldenNet never talks over the game’s own dialogue, and a line counts as said only once you have seen it.

In the design: DESIGN §8 · O45

Memory

Memory of you and of each other

Every character with a profile keeps a short journal: who you are to them, and a handful of keepsakes about you and about the other characters they deal with. How they feel about you moves a little with each conversation and is shown in words, never as a number. Memories follow your save: loading an earlier one rolls them back with it, and each new cycle of the game starts a journey of its own.

In the design: DESIGN §10.2 · DESIGN §10.5 · O45

Profiles

Character profiles and the roster

Profiles come at three depths. The characters with the longest stories get a persona that follows their story step by step; every other named character and named boss gets a character profile, a general portrait in our own words; and soldiers, wanderers and their like get a card for their kind. The whole base-game roster ships with version 1.0. You can add your own instructions to any profile, or write one of your own for a character you have met.

In the design: DESIGN §7.7 · DESIGN §7.8 · O43 · O46

Awareness

Quest and world awareness

Important characters know where their own quest stands, the quests tied to it, and the stage the world is in, read from the game’s own records once each has been verified. They know only what they could have learnt: what happened where they stand, what the whole land saw, and word that travels along the road. News can come up in conversation when it touches them; what they have not heard of stays out of their mouths.

In the design: DESIGN §10.6 · O51

Speaking up

Speaking up, within pacing

Characters start lines themselves when they have a reason: a greeting when you come near, quest business, news, a remark, an answer to someone else, or a subject they like to talk about. There is no quota of lines. What holds them back is the pacing you set (a gap between lines, a window, a cooldown for each kind of line), what each type of character may do, and safe moments.

In the design: DESIGN §8.9 · DESIGN §9.4 · O31 · O39

Combat

Fights and status effects

Every fighter can speak, on your side or against you, and so can characters fighting each other, boss fights included; a line never plays over the game’s own scripted lines. Characters also know their own status effects and see each other’s, and react in words: a warning, a plea for help, fear. Each status joins once its signal in the game has been verified.

In the design: DESIGN §9.2 · DESIGN §9.9 · DESIGN §9.10 · O40 · O42

Exchanges

Characters talk to each other

Two characters on the same side who meet can greet each other and trade a few lines, close enough for you to overhear. Characters also answer what others say near them, so a camp or a crossing feels lived in without you at the centre of it.

In the design: DESIGN §9.7 · O34

Generic characters

Everyone with a voice knows what they are

Soldiers, militia and wanderers know what they are, where they are and what is happening in a fight, and say short lines in character. Their lines start from written seeds that need no network at all, widened by lines prepared ahead in the background, so a fight never waits on a model.

In the design: DESIGN §7.4 · DESIGN §9.3

Spoilers

Spoilers kept out by what is said, not by bans

Each character is told the present as it stands for you, in plain positive terms, rather than handed a list of things not to mention, which would only put them in mind. Every line is checked against what is still locked for you before it is shown, and a record that has not been verified can hide something but never reveal it. The menu keeps the same rule: nothing you have not reached is shown, counted or hinted at.

In the design: DESIGN §10.4 · O36

Events

An event engine of its own

Elden Ring has no event system a mod can listen to, so EldenNet has its own, Augur. Four times a second it compares what it sees of the world, watches the game’s records, and turns what changed into events (someone came near, a fight began, a character fell), each with the list of those who could perceive it. Every reaction, memory and line starts from those events, and recorded play replays through Augur in the tests.

In the design: DESIGN §6 · O49

Stability

Gentle on your game

EldenNet is one small native library with its own launcher. It needs no mod loader, though you may load it through one you already use. It changes no game code, writes nothing to the game’s memory and never touches your save file; its launcher keeps backups of your saves all the same. An unknown game version keeps EldenNet asleep, and a check that fails at start turns the part concerned off with a notice, instead of guessing.

In the design: DESIGN §3 · DESIGN §4.4 · O47 · O48

Privacy and safety

  • Offline only. EldenNet works only while you play offline, alone in your own world, with no anti-cheat running; otherwise it stays dormant.
  • Your conversations stay yours. Nothing is sent anywhere but to the model service you chose; what EldenNet measures stays on your PC and is never uploaded.
  • Keys go in and never come out. A key you enter is never shown again, never written to a log and never put in a bug report.
  • The browser dashboard is for this PC. It answers this PC only, unless you switch on your home network and pair a device with a code shown in game, and it refuses requests from other web pages.
  • Nothing personal in reports. Logs and bug reports leave out your keys, your name, your folders and your addresses.
  • The game’s own files stay the game’s. EldenNet ships no game text, art or audio; it reads what it needs from your own install while you play.
  • Built openly with AI assistance.

The menu: GraceUI

GraceUI is EldenNet’s menu and dashboard: the same pages in game on a hotkey, and in your browser on the same PC while the game runs. It wears its own dark theme, Grace: warm ash greys lit by a single gold light. These are screenshots of the design mock, with placeholder data and invented names; the menu itself is built alongside EldenNet’s versions.

Where it stands

Design stage. Nothing is built yet. The design of version 1 and its review rounds are done, and so is the design of GraceUI; the owner has made every decision but two (O46 and O54). Two things wait on the owner: Min’s engine blueprint, SkyrimNet’s game-independent core, which becomes EldenNet’s foundation and comes before the build, and the structure of the character profile (decision O46), which comes before the roster is written. The other open decision, O54, is whether a first count of the roster, in numbers only, may start before both.

Then the engine is adopted, the roster is written in batches, and EldenNet’s versions follow, from v0.1 Eyes to v1.0, each one ready for testers, with GraceUI’s menu growing beside them.
